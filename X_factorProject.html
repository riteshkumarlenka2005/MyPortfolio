Section Architecture

Every section analyzed from the perspective of FAANG recruiters, startup founders, hiring managers, senior engineers, and design-conscious visitors.

Position 01 · First Viewport
Hero
● Essential
"You have 4 seconds. Either the visitor understands exactly who you are and feels something — or they leave. The Hero is not decoration. It is the entire argument for why this person matters."
Word Count
15 – 30 words
Screen Height
100vh
Load Priority
Highest
Recruiter Time
3 – 6 sec
Mandatory Content
  • Your full name — large, confident, unmissable
  • A single-line identity statement ("AI/ML Engineer & Full Stack Developer")
  • One punchy tagline that signals your philosophy, not your skill list
  • Primary CTA — "View Work" or "See Projects"
  • Secondary CTA — "Download Resume" or "Contact Me"
What to Avoid
  • Listing 10 technologies in the hero — this signals insecurity, not competence
  • Generic taglines: "Passionate developer," "Problem solver"
  • Auto-playing videos that slow the first paint
  • Carousels or sliders in the hero
  • Any text below 18px font size
  • More than 2 CTAs
Recommended Layout

Centered layout with your name as the dominant typographic element (80–120px). Identity line directly below in a contrasting weight. Tagline in a subdued color. CTAs left-aligned or centered. Optional: ambient 3D particle system or subtle starfield as background — never covering the text. Your current Three.js starfield is an asset — keep it, but ensure text contrast is WCAG AA compliant.

Suggested CTAs
View My Work → Download Resume Let's Connect
Position 02 · Immediately After Hero
About
● Essential
"After the Hero creates curiosity, About must answer the question: Who is this person, really? Not their resume — their mind. Recruiters don't read About sections to verify facts. They read them to decide if they like this person."
Word Count
80 – 120 words
Screen Height
60–80vh
Tone
Personal + Professional
Perspective
First Person
Mandatory Content
  • Your philosophy or worldview — not your skills
  • What drives you (curiosity, depth, building — from your own words)
  • Domain clarity: AI/ML + Full Stack
  • One line that differentiates you from 10,000 other developers
  • Subtle personality — enough to make them want to meet you
What to Avoid
  • "I am a passionate developer who loves coding"
  • Repeating your resume in prose
  • Listing skills in the About section — skills have their own section
  • Talking about your college name or CGPA here
  • Writing more than 150 words — this kills readability
What Recruiters Actually Look For Here

Communication ability. Can this person articulate their value clearly? Personality fit. Is this someone the team can work with? Self-awareness. Does this person understand their own strengths and why they matter? They are not checking facts — they are checking signals.

Suggested CTAs
Read Full Story → View Projects
Position 03 · After About
Skills / Tech Stack
● Recommended
"Skills sections are almost always done wrong. They are not lists of every technology you've ever touched. They are a curated signal of your technical depth. Less is more. Grouped is better. Shown through work is best."
Word Count
Labels Only
Screen Height
40–60vh
Format
Grouped Tags
Max Skills
20–25
Mandatory Content
  • Group by domain: AI/ML · Web Dev · Languages · Tools
  • Only technologies you can confidently discuss in an interview
  • Visual grouping — not a single flat list
  • Optional: proficiency indicators (but avoid percentage bars — they are subjective and often misread)
What to Avoid
  • Skill percentage bars ("Python — 90%") — no recruiter trusts these
  • Listing 40+ technologies — signals inability to prioritize
  • Including skills like "Microsoft Word" or "Google Docs"
  • Unordered dump of logos without grouping
Recruiter Psychology

FAANG recruiters scan for keywords matching their JD. Startup founders look for overlap with their stack. Both are spending under 10 seconds here. Your goal: make it scannable in 5 seconds and let the projects prove the depth.

Position 04 · The Core of the Portfolio
Featured Projects
● Essential
"This is the only section recruiters truly study. Everything else creates context. Projects create proof. This section can get you hired — or eliminate you — on its own."
Projects to Show
3 – 5 max
Words Per Project
40 – 80
Screen Height
100–140vh
Recruiter Time
30 – 90 sec
Mandatory Per Project
  • Project name + one-line purpose (what problem it solves)
  • Tech stack used — clearly labeled
  • Your specific role and contribution
  • A quantifiable outcome or notable feature
  • Live link and/or GitHub link
  • Visual — screenshot, mockup, or demo GIF
What to Avoid
  • Showing 10+ projects — too many signals low curation ability
  • Projects without live links or GitHub — signals incomplete work
  • Vague descriptions: "Built an AI app using machine learning"
  • Tutorial clone projects (todo apps, weather apps)
  • Projects with no visual representation
Recommended Layout

Featured project at full-width. Remaining 3–4 in a 2-column grid. Each card should show: visual thumbnail, project name, domain tags, 2-line description, and action links. On hover: subtle lift or overlay with more detail. Your "Holistic Interview Intelligence" and "CyberGuardian AI" are your two strongest projects — lead with those.

What FAANG Recruiters Specifically Look For

Complexity, ownership, and real deployment. They want to see: Did you build something hard? Did you own it end-to-end? Does it actually work live? Your 5+ deployed apps are a significant differentiator — make each one impossible to miss.

Suggested CTAs
View Live → GitHub → See All Projects

03

Content Strategy Report

What recruiters actually read, what they skip, and how to engineer every word for maximum impact.

What Recruiters Actually Read
  • 01 Your name, title, and tagline (Hero) — always
  • 02 Featured project names and live links — always
  • 03 Project descriptions if the title intrigued them
  • 04 Your About section — if they're already interested
  • 05 Tech stack — scanned for keyword matches
  • 06 Contact details — once they've decided to reach out
What Recruiters Usually Skip
  • ✕ Long paragraphs inside project descriptions
  • ✕ Certifications — unless they're from Google, AWS, etc.
  • ✕ Detailed timelines with minor events
  • ✕ Skills sections that are just logo grids
  • ✕ Footer links they didn't click on purpose
  • ✕ Any block of text exceeding 5 lines
Sections That Create Strongest Impressions
  • 01 Hero — the visual design tone is set here instantly
  • 02 Projects — your deployed, live apps are your strongest asset
  • 03 About — if well-written, it makes them root for you
  • 04 Achievements — specific numbers create instant credibility
Ideal Content Density
  • ▸ Hero: 15–30 words total, maximum
  • ▸ About: 80–120 words — one strong paragraph
  • ▸ Each project card: 40–80 words
  • ▸ Achievement items: 1 line with a number
  • ▸ Rule: If you can cut it without losing meaning — cut it
  • ▸ Too much text signals inability to prioritize

The single most common mistake in AI/ML developer portfolios: they show what they know instead of what they've built. Recruiters are not academics — they don't care about your theoretical understanding of transformers. They care that you built and shipped something that uses them. Every section should answer one implicit question: "What has this person actually made?"

Modern 2026 Portfolio Blueprint

The final recommended section order from top to bottom, with exact specifications for each.

01
Hero
Height: 100vh
Animation: Ambient starfield or particle system
Priority: Name → Title → Tagline → CTAs
Full viewport. Name dominates. Identity line second. Tagline subtle. Two CTAs only. Your Three.js background is your signature — preserve it. Ensure all text passes WCAG AA contrast.
02
About (Brief — Home Version)
Height: 60–80vh
Animation: Scroll-triggered fade-in
Priority: Paragraph → Portrait → CTA to Full About
One powerful paragraph. Optional: portrait photo in premium frame. CTA linking to the full About page. This is where your "Version 1" paragraph lives — don't modify it, it's strong.
03
Skills / Tech Stack
Height: 40–60vh
Animation: Staggered tag reveal on scroll
Priority: Domain Group → Technology Tags
Grouped by domain: AI/ML, Web Development, Languages, Tools. Tag-based layout. No bars, no percentages. Scannable in under 5 seconds.
04
Featured Projects
Height: 120–160vh
Animation: Card lift on hover, parallax on scroll
Priority: Visual → Name → Tags → Description → Links
Lead with Holistic Interview Intelligence (full width). Grid of 3–4 remaining projects below. Each card: visual, name, stack tags, 2-line description, live + GitHub links. This is the make-or-break section.
05
Achievements
Height: 40–60vh
Animation: Count-up numbers on scroll entry
Priority: Big Number → Context Label
4–5 stat cards with large, impactful numbers. "9.82 CGPA · University Topper," "5+ Live Apps," "₹10,000 Prize." Count-up animation on scroll adds drama without gimmick.
06
Experience & Timeline
Height: 60–80vh
Animation: Line draw on scroll, entry fade
Priority: Date → Event → Brief Description
Vertical timeline. 5–7 entries maximum. Internship, SIH 2025, first deployment, University Topper, Science Exhibition win. Your GSAP rope physics timeline is your signature — if it's performant, keep it.
07
Certifications (Compact)
Height: 30–40vh
Animation: Fade on scroll
Priority: Issuer → Course Name → Year
Minimal grid. 4–5 certifications max. Small, quiet, visually understated. Should not compete visually with projects or achievements.
08
Contact
Height: 60–80vh
Animation: Subtle glow on CTA hover
Priority: Warm Headline → Form → Social Links
Warm, minimal, frictionless. One headline. Email + LinkedIn + GitHub displayed visibly. Contact form optional. One human line that says you actually respond to messages.
05

Portfolio Critique Checklist

55 checkpoints covering every dimension of a world-class portfolio. Audit your portfolio against each before launch.

Typography
Hero name uses a display font at 80px+ size
No more than 2 typeface families used across the entire site
Body text is minimum 15px with 1.65–1.8 line-height
Monospace font used for technical labels, dates, and code
Heading hierarchy is clear: H1 → H2 → H3 never reversed
Letter-spacing on uppercase labels: 0.1–0.2em
Layout & Spacing
Section padding consistent: minimum 80px top and bottom
Max content width capped at 960–1100px for readability
No section bleeds into the next without a clear visual break
Grid columns collapse properly at 768px and 480px breakpoints
White space is generous — content never feels cramped
Visual hierarchy leads the eye naturally top to bottom
Visual Design & Aesthetic
Color palette is consistent — maximum 2 accent colors
Background texture or pattern is subtle, never distracting
All cards use consistent border-radius or sharp edges — not mixed
Project visuals are high quality — no blurry screenshots
The design feels premium at first glance — not like a template
There is one signature visual element that is uniquely yours
Performance & Technical
First Contentful Paint (FCP) under 1.5 seconds
3D/WebGL assets do not block the main thread on load
Images are WebP format and lazy-loaded
Lighthouse Performance score above 85
All live project links are functional and not broken
GitHub links open in new tab and are correct repos
Mobile performance tested on real device, not just DevTools
Responsiveness
Portfolio is fully functional at 375px (iPhone SE) width
Navigation collapses to a clean hamburger or drawer on mobile
No horizontal scroll on any screen size
Touch targets (buttons, links) are minimum 44px on mobile
3D animations fallback gracefully on low-power mobile devices
Font sizes scale properly — no tiny text on small screens
Accessibility
All text meets WCAG AA contrast ratio (4.5:1 minimum)
All images have descriptive alt text
Keyboard navigation works through all interactive elements
Animations respect prefers-reduced-motion media query
Focus states are visible for all interactive elements
Recruiter Psychology
Recruiter understands your role within 3 seconds of landing
Your best project is visible without scrolling on desktop
Resume is downloadable with one click from the Hero or nav
Every project has both live link and GitHub — no exceptions
Contact email is visible without any form submission required
No wall of text anywhere — recruiters skim, not read
Social proof (University Topper, live apps) is visible early
Content Quality
Zero spelling or grammatical errors across the entire portfolio
No generic phrases: "passionate," "hardworking," "team player"
Each project description answers: what, why, how, and outcome
About section sounds like a human, not a resume
All numbers are specific: "500+ students," "5+ apps" — not vague
Content is timeless — no references to current semester or year
Premium Aesthetic Principles
The portfolio has a clear, unique visual identity — not a template
Animations serve the content, not the other way around
Empty space is used intentionally — not filled out of anxiety
The portfolio feels "art-directed" — not assembled
Cursor, scrollbar, or selection color is customized to match brand
The loading screen (if any) is fast and branded

The goal is not to impress everyone. It is to be impossible to ignore by the right people.
